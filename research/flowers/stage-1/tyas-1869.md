## Approved inclusion rules

A retained record must pass both gates:

1. **Flower eligibility:** ordinarily perceived as a flower, or a prominent flowering ornamental/wild/medicinal plant. Classic ornamental flowers and explicit blossom entries remain eligible. Symbolism based on another feature does not disqualify an otherwise eligible flower.
2. **Narrative adequacy:** an owned main-text article must explain the flower–sentiment link or give concrete historical, cultural, mythological, traditional, etymological, botanical, or event context. A terminal-list equation by itself is insufficient.

Trees, grains, grasses, crops, culinary herbs, foliage-oriented woody plants, succulents, wreaths, garlands, bouquets/nosegays, and mixed arrangements are excluded.

The final consistency review restored `Acanthus` and `A Rose Leaf`: Acanthus has an adequate arts/architecture rationale and prominent flower display, while the rose-leaf meaning is allowed because rose is ordinarily perceived as a flower. It removed earlier false positives including acacia as a whole tree, aloe, balm, basil, bramble, broom, flax, hawthorn, heath, hop, horse chestnut, ivy, laurustinus, lucerne, madder, motherwort, myrtle, nightshade, privet, rosemary, cactus, thyme, weeping willow, wormwood, and garland presentations.

## Extraction and verification method

1. IA DjVu OCR/XML was used only to discover candidate rows and page structure.
2. Every terminal-list row on printed pp. 301–312 was checked against the scan images, including inherited dash headings, punctuation, sentiment wording, and row order.
3. Main-text context was reconciled against printed index pp. 313–318 and the corresponding main-entry headings.
4. Article ownership was bounded by sentiment and botanical headings in reading order. A named figure or shared page was not enough without source-specific context inside the entry boundary.
5. Only rows with an adequate main-text treatment survived final cleanup; the 112 terminal-list-only rows were removed.
6. Historical spellings and inherited source headings remain unchanged; normalized names are non-authoritative research labels.

## Illustration assessment and validation

The 1869 preface says that eight coloured groups by James Andrews were introduced. They are group compositions, not one-to-one botanical portraits. Non-exact plate/list matches remain marked uncertain. `botanical_image_missing` records whether a standalone botanical image asset was acquired, not whether the source has any illustration.

Final validation confirms **137 unique record IDs**, nonblank required evidence fields, `classification=include` on every row, owned main-text context on every row, and valid direct scan-page URLs. Source anomalies remain preserved rather than silently corrected.
